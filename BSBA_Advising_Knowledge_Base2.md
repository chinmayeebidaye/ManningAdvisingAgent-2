## Section 3: Math Placement (ALEKS Test) & Core Requirement Notes

### Math Placement — ALEKS Test

*(Applies to MATH.1210 in both pathways and only within Manning School of Business advising.)*

MATH.1210 (Management Precalculus) has no listed course prerequisite. Incoming/continuing Manning students who have **not completed MATH.1210** and have no transfer/AP equivalent may use their **ALEKS placement test score** to place directly into a math course.

| ALEKS Score | Placement Result |
|---|:---:|
| 00–29 | MATH.1115: Fundamentals of Algebra |
| 30–100 | MATH.1210: Management Precalculus |
| 55–100* | MATH.1220: Management Calculus (skips MATH.1210) |

\*A score of **55–100** allows the student to skip MATH.1210 and place directly into MATH.1220.

### Notes

- The ALEKS placement option applies **only to Manning School of Business advising**. Do not suggest this placement pathway for non-Manning courses.
- ALEKS placement is relevant when the student has not completed or is not currently enrolled in MATH.1210 and has no equivalent transfer/AP credit or other documented placement.
- The ALEKS test Is an option before defaulting to MATH.1210 in the plan.
- A score of **55 or higher** allows direct placement into MATH.1220 and therefore allows the student to skip MATH.1210.
- A score of **30–54** places the student into MATH.1210.
- A score of **0–29** places the student into MATH.1115.
- Do not assume or invent an ALEKS score.
- If the student has not taken ALEKS or does not know their score, MATH.1210 may be used as the default planning course while noting that ALEKS placement could allow accelerated placement.
- Testing and placement details are available through UMass Lowell's placement testing information: https://www.uml.edu/orientation/placement-testing/testing-placements.aspx

## Section 4: Concentration Requirements

*(Same for both Fall 2026 and Beyond and Prior (Before Fall 2026) Pathway students.)*
### Concentration Detection Logic
Do not rely on the short label at the top of the advisement report, it is display-only and may be incomplete.

Step 1: Locate concentration mentions
Search the full advisement report text, case-insensitive, for the string "concentrat" and review every occurrence. A student may have two named concentrations, either joined in one header or as two separate headers. Ignore hits such as "Concentration Elective" that are not concentration name headers.

Step 2: Extract the real name(s)
The actual concentration name(s) appear as bold headers in the body of the report (not the summary label). A single header may contain two joined concentration names - do not assume it's only one. For example: "ACCOUNTING AND FINANCE CONCENTRATION" would be two separate concentrations 1. Accounting and 2. Finance. Always should be treated separate.

Step 3: Parse joined headers
For each header found:
Strip the "MANNING" prefix and "CONCENTRATION" suffix.
Do not split on "AND": "Analytics and Operations Management" contains "and" in its own name. Instead, scan the remaining text for the official names below, longest name first, removing each name from the text once it is found: Analytics and Operations Management; Management Information Systems; International Business; Entrepreneurship; Accounting; Marketing; Finance; Management. (Longest-first keeps Management Information Systems and Analytics and Operations Management from being read as Management.)
Legacy name: "Supply Chain" or "Supply Chain & Operations Management" is the Prior-era name for Analytics and Operations Management (same POMS.4010, POMS.4020, POMS.4030, POMS.4040 and POMS elective block). Count it as Analytics and Operations Management, never as a third concentration.
Any leftover text that is not a separator (AND, &, /, +, comma) is an unrecognized fragment: flag it and ask the student.

**Official Concentration List:**
1. Accounting
2. Management
3. Marketing
4. Entrepreneurship
5. Management Information Systems
6. Analytics and Operations Management
7. Finance
8. International Business

Step 4: Handle matches
If both fragments match the official list → the student has two concentrations. List both. Never default to only the final fragment.
Run a full, independent requirements check for each concentration identified, using its determined pathway.

Step 5: Confirm with student
before finalizing, state the detected concentration count and name(s) back to the student for confirmation.

The concentration requirements for each pathway are listed below.

### Concentration Elective Structure (Total Load Rules)

Every concentration requires **5 required core concentration courses**, EXCEPT Accounting (8 required core courses) and Finance (7 required core courses). In addition to the concentration-specific core courses, a fixed set of **general elective slots** fills out the remaining credits toward the 120-credit floor. Use this table when planning electives for ANY concentration (single or dual) and when advising Undeclared students on what elective load to expect once they pick a concentration.

**Standard Concentrations** (all except Accounting and Finance — i.e., Management, Marketing, Entrepreneurship, MIS, Analytics and Operations Management, International Business): **5 required core concentration courses + 8 elective slots** (two Manning/Non-Manning, four Manning Elective (3000/4000 level), two Manning/Non-Manning (3000/4000 level)) as listed below:

| # | Elective Slot |
|---|---|
| 1 | Manning or Non-Manning Elective |
| 2 | Manning or Non-Manning Elective |
| 3 | Manning Elective (3000/4000 level) |
| 4 | Manning Elective (3000/4000 level) |
| 5 | Manning Elective (3000/4000 level) |
| 6 | Manning Elective (3000/4000 level) |
| 7 | Manning or Non-Manning Elective (3000/4000 level) |
| 8 | Manning or Non-Manning Elective (3000/4000 level) |

**Accounting Concentration (single):** **8 required core concentration courses + 5 elective slots** (two Manning/Non-Manning, one Manning Elective (3000/4000 level), two Manning/Non-Manning (3000/4000 level)) , per the Accounting pathway in Part B, Section 1).

**Finance Concentration (single):** **7 required core concentration courses + 6 elective slots** (two Manning/Non-Manning, two Manning Elective (3000/4000 level), two Manning/Non-Manning (3000/4000 level)) same total elective load as the standard pattern above minus the courses already fulfilled by the Finance core. ACCT.3300 and ACCT.4010 are pre-approved substitutes for either of the two "FINA Elective (3000/4000 level)" core slots.

**Dual Concentration — Accounting AND Finance ONLY:** When a student is completing BOTH Accounting and Finance, do not check the two concentrations as fully independent 8-course and 7-course sets layered with separate elective pools. Because ACCT.3300 and ACCT.4010 are approved FINA Electives, the two required-course lists overlap:
- Total required concentration courses for the dual pathway = **13** (not 15): the 8 Accounting core courses (which already include ACCT.3300 and ACCT.4010) plus the 5 non-overlapping Finance-specific courses (FINA.3110, FINA.3210, FINA.3310, FINA.4410, FINA.4910).
- The two "FINA Elective (3000/4000 level)" slots are satisfied by ACCT.3300 and ACCT.4010 (already required by Accounting) — do NOT double-count these as separate electives, and do NOT ask the student to take 2 additional FINA electives on top.
- Confirm this dual substitution explicitly with the student before finalizing the plan, and still run a full independent check that every one of the 13 courses is completed or scheduled.
- General elective slots for this dual pathway follow the Accounting single-concentration elective count (5 slots), since Finance's required courses are already fully absorbed into the 13.

### Undeclared (No Concentration Declared)

Applies when the "concentrat" search in the advisement report returns **zero matches** (no bold concentration header found), or the student states they have not declared a concentration.

- Undeclared students have **no concentration-specific course requirements** yet. Do not check them against any concentration table above.
- Remaining requirements should be reported only for BOK, Collateral, Foundation, Core, and Senior Core - omit the Concentration line entirely (or mark it "Undeclared - not yet applicable").
- For semester planning, prioritize:
  1. All still-needed BOK/Collateral/Foundation/Core courses (these apply regardless of eventual concentration).
  2. "Gateway"/introductory courses from a few concentrations the student is considering, so coursework doubles as exploration.
  3. General elective slots - label these generically as **"Concentration Elective"** rather than locking them to a specific department prefix, since the exact structure depends on which concentration the student eventually picks.
- Default explanation for planning purposes: assume the standard **5 concentration courses + 8 electives** pattern (this covers Management, Marketing, Entrepreneurship, MIS, Analytics and Operations Management, and International Business). Only mention Accounting or Finance's different course counts (8+5 and 7+6 respectively, or 13 total if pairing Accounting+Finance) if the student expresses interest in one of those two concentrations specifically - otherwise there's no need to walk through every pattern.
- Proactively note any university deadline or credit threshold for declaring a concentration if the report or student mentions one, and recommend the student meet with an advisor to declare before it affects registration.

## Section 5: Honors College Requirements

*(Applies only to students enrolled in the Honors College, in addition to — not instead of — their Manning BOK/Collateral/Foundation/Core/Senior Core/Concentration requirements above. Applies to both Fall 2026 and Beyond and Prior (Before Fall 2026) Pathway students.)*

### General Requirements

To complete the Honors College requirements and graduate as a **Commonwealth Honors Scholar**, honors students must:

- Maintain a **3.250 cumulative GPA**
- Complete the Honors curriculum requirements (H1–H8)
- Complete at least **one approved Enrichment requirement (E1)** for each semester the student is a member of the Honors College
- Complete and defend an **Honors Project or Honors Thesis**

To complete the Honors College requirements and graduate with the **Honors College Distinction**, honors students must:

- Maintain a **3.250 cumulative GPA**
- Complete the Honors curriculum requirements **excluding the Honors Project**
- Complete at least **one approved Enrichment requirement (E1)** for each semester the student is a member of the Honors College
- Be a member of the Honors College during the **semester of graduation**

### Honors Curriculum Requirements

| # | Requirement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| HONORS 1 (H1) | First Year Seminar in Honors (FYSH) | If necessary, may be replaced by a dedicated honors AH/SS core curriculum course. |
| HONORS 2 (H2) | Honors College Writing II | If the student already has credit for non-honors College Writing II, may be replaced by a dedicated honors AH/SS core curriculum course. |
| HONORS 3 (H3) | Upper-Level Honors Seminar | Must be a specific upper-level honors seminar course/experience. Not satisfiable by a regular 3000/4000-level elective. Verify against the student's report. |
| HONORS 4 (H4) | Honors Course / Experiential Learning | Either a dedicated honors course OR an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity. |
| HONORS 5 (H5) | Honors Course / Honors-by-Contract / Experiential Learning | Either a dedicated honors course, an Honors-by-Contract on a non-honors 3000/4000-level course, OR an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity. |
| HONORS 6 (H6) | Honors Course / Honors-by-Contract / Experiential Learning / Graduate Course | Either a dedicated honors 2000/3000/4000-level course, an Honors-by-Contract on a non-honors 3000/4000-level course, an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity, OR any graduate-level course. |
| HONORS 7 (H7) | Honors Project/Thesis Preparation | Either a course supporting the first half of a two-semester Honors Project or Thesis, a 3000/4000-level dedicated honors course, OR a graduate course. |
| HONORS 8 (H8) | Honors Project/Thesis Completion | Either an upper-level letter-graded course supporting the second half of a two-semester Honors Project/Thesis OR an upper-level letter-graded course supporting a one-semester Honors Project. |

**Notes:**
- Honors College requirements apply only to students enrolled in the Honors College.
- **H1:** First Year Seminar in Honors (FYSH). If necessary, this may be replaced by a dedicated honors AH/SS core curriculum course.
- **H2:** Honors College Writing II. If the student already has non-honors College Writing II credit, this may be replaced by a dedicated honors AH/SS core curriculum course.
- **H3:** Must be an upper-level Honors Seminar. A regular 3000/4000-level elective does not satisfy this requirement.
- **H4:** May be satisfied by either a dedicated honors course or an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity.
- **H5:** May be satisfied by a dedicated honors course, an Honors-by-Contract on a non-honors 3000/4000-level course, or an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity.
- **H6:** May be satisfied by a dedicated honors 2000/3000/4000-level course, an Honors-by-Contract on a non-honors 3000/4000-level course, an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity, or any graduate-level course.
- For Commonwealth Honors, no more than **two (2) Honors Experiential Learning Opportunities** may be used to satisfy H4/H5/H6.
- **H7:** May be satisfied by a course supporting the first half of a two-semester Honors Project/Thesis, a 3000/4000-level dedicated honors course, or a graduate course.
- **H8:** Must be an upper-level letter-graded course supporting either the second half of a two-semester Honors Project/Thesis or a one-semester Honors Project.
- H3, H7, and H8 are distinct honors/project requirements and are not automatically satisfied by generic electives. If the student's planned course does not clearly satisfy the stated requirement, it should be confirmed with Honors College advisor.
- For **H4**, an eligible course may be a Manning or non-Manning course, but it must be a dedicated Honors course or an Honors Experiential Learning Opportunity.
- For **H5/H6**, an eligible course may be a Manning or non-Manning course. When using an Honors-by-Contract, the student must confirm the “Honors-By-Contract” with the course faculty and complete the necessary paperwork with the Honors College.
- Honors requirements are **in addition to**, not replacements for, Manning degree requirements. However, certain Manning courses may satisfy Honors requirements when they meet the applicable H1–H8 criteria.
- H7/H8 timing should be considered carefully, particularly when an Honors Project/Thesis is expected during the senior year.
- The Honors Project/Thesis is separate from the Manning Capstone (MGMT.4900/ENTR.4960).
- For Honors students, "Remaining Requirements" should include a separate **"Honors College Requirements"** subsection listing H1–H8 individually.

## Section 7: Scenario Templates

*(Reusable response templates referenced by agent instructions. The instructions file determines WHEN each template is used; this section defines the template STRUCTURE and content to fill in.)*

### 7.1 What-If Scenario Template

Use this structure when a student asks a hypothetical question about how a change would affect their plan (e.g., "what if I switch from Marketing to Finance," "what if I fail this prerequisite," "what if I take a semester off," "what if I add a second concentration").

**Template:**

1. **Restate the scenario** in one sentence to confirm understanding.
2. **Baseline vs. hypothetical** — briefly state what the student's current plan looks like (from their advisement report / stated progress), then what changes under the hypothetical.
3. **Requirement impact** — walk through which requirement categories are affected (BOK/Collateral/Foundation/Core/Senior Core, Concentration, CCE, Honors if applicable), citing the relevant Section(s) of Part A and/or Part B.
4. **Prerequisite/sequencing impact** — check Part C (Course Data & Prerequisites) for any chain effects the hypothetical would trigger (e.g., a delayed course pushing back everything downstream of it).
5. **Timeline impact** — state plainly whether the change adds semesters, and if so roughly how many, using Part D (Manning Course Schedule) to confirm course availability under the new sequence.
6. **Caveat** — note that this is a planning estimate, not a guarantee, and that formal changes (concentration switch, leave of absence, etc.) need to go through the actual university process/advisor.

### 7.1a Concentration Change Scenarios (switch, add a second, or compare)

Use this procedure, together with the 7.1 template, whenever a student asks "what if I choose / switch to X," "what if I add Y," or "compare X and Y." The goal is a plan built on the TARGET concentration's real course list from Section 4, never generic placeholders such as "Management Concentration Course."

1. **Identify the target(s).** Match the student's wording to the Official Concentration List in Section 4 ("Supply Chain" means Analytics and Operations Management). A switch replaces the current concentration. "Add" creates two concentrations (apply the dual-concentration rules in Section 4). "Compare" means build each option separately. If it is unclear which is meant, ask one question.
2. **Keep fixed:** the pathway version, completed and in-progress courses, credit totals, GPA and Honors status. Do not re-run concentration detection on the report; show the report's concentration(s) and the scenario side by side (for example, "Report: Undeclared | Scenario: Management").
3. **Pull the target's required courses** from its "[Name] Concentration" table in Section 4 and list them by code and title. Use the same pathway version's pathway for that concentration in Part B to see where those courses normally fall.
4. **Apply the load structure** from the Concentration Elective Structure in Section 4: standard concentrations are 5 required courses + 8 elective slots, Accounting is 8 + 5, Finance is 7 + 6, and Accounting + Finance together is 13.
5. **Map what the student has already taken.** Mark each required concentration course Done / In progress / Remaining. A completed course can fill an elective slot only if it meets that slot's rule (for example, a Manning 3000/4000-level course fits a "Manning Elective (3000/4000 level)" slot). Never count one course as both required and elective. When switching away from a declared concentration, list the old concentration's courses already taken and which open elective slot each can fill; if no slot is open, flag the extra credits and advise confirming with ManningSchoolofBusiness@uml.edu.
6. **Build the semester plan:** remaining core + the target's remaining required courses + the elective slots, with prerequisites checked in Part C and capstone in the final semester. Part D covers terms through Spring 2027. For later terms, use the Fall/Spring pattern shown in Part D (a course offered only in Spring recently goes in a Spring term) and label those placements "availability unconfirmed until the schedule is released."
7. **Format of the answer:** use the Full Report Format from the agent instructions. Label the concentration section "[Name] (scenario)". In the remaining-requirements section, show a table of the target's required courses with their status and a table of the elective slots with the course filling each or "Open". In the semester plan, name actual course codes for every concentration course; elective slots may be labeled by slot type (for example, "Manning Elective (3000/4000 level)"). In the Email Summary, use the scenario concentration's name as the heading and add "(scenario)".
8. **State the impact versus the baseline** in two sentences: how many courses remain and the estimated graduation term, compared with the student's current plan.
9. **Compare mode:** use one table with a column per option and these rows: required courses (codes), how many are already done or in progress, how many remain, elective load, prerequisite chains or gateway courses (Part C), season constraints (Part D), and earliest graduation term. Use only facts from this knowledge base. Never rank concentrations or make claims about salary, job demand, hiring, career outcomes, or which is "best." If asked which to choose, say it depends on the student's interests and goals, summarize the factual differences, and suggest speaking with an advisor. Then offer to build a full plan for any one option.
10. **Caveat:** this is a planning estimate; a real concentration change goes through the advisor and university process.

### 7.2 Email Draft Template

Use this structure when a student asks for help drafting an email (e.g., to a professor, advisor, or department) about a scheduling, prerequisite, or requirement issue surfaced during advising.

**Template:**

1. **Subject line** — short and specific (e.g., "Question about ACCT.3030 prerequisite for Fall 2027").
2. **Greeting** — appropriately formal for a student-to-faculty/staff email.
3. **Context** — 1–2 sentences on who the student is (year, concentration) and what prompted the email.
4. **The actual ask** — a clear, specific question or request, not a vague description of the problem.
5. **Supporting detail** — only what's needed for the recipient to act (e.g., course codes, planned semester, relevant requirement) — pull this from the plan already discussed, don't re-derive it.
6. **Closing** — thank the recipient, sign off with the student's name.
7. **Tone note for the agent:** keep drafts concise (under ~150 words) and let the student fill in/adjust personal specifics rather than inventing them.

## Graduating, credits and GPA

### E.1 How do I apply to graduate? What is the DIG (Declaration of Intent to Graduate)?

**Answer:**
The Declaration of Intent to Graduate (DIG) is the form that starts your graduation review. Submit it during the semester before your final semester (after you register for your final-semester courses and before that final semester begins). A Manning professional advisor then checks that your remaining courses will complete your program.

Steps:
1. Check your advisement report in SiS (use Expand All) and plan your final semester with your faculty advisor.
2. If you need to add or drop a concentration, do that first with the Change of Concentration form (https://www.uml.edu/thesolutioncenter/forms/program-changes/msb.aspx). It takes 1-2 days to show on your report, and a change can alter which courses you need, so re-check the report.
3. Register for your final-semester courses. Because that is your last semester, you can enter "N/A" in the "Future Enrollment" box on the form.
4. Submit the Manning DIG form, which is linked on https://www.uml.edu/Catalog/Undergraduate/Business/Policies/Declaration-of-Program.aspx. Example: a student finishing in Spring registers for Spring courses and submits the DIG during the Fall.
5. A professional advisor reviews it (usually a few weeks) and emails you. Either your courses are confirmed or any discrepancies are listed. Reply to confirm; after that you are placed on the graduation list.

Good to know:
- Manning emails students with at least 85 earned credits when it is time to submit. If you did not see it, search your email for "DIG".
- Summer graduates get a separate invitation and submit once summer registration opens.
- Skipping or delaying the DIG can lead to the wrong course selections, a delay in your diploma, and your name missing from the commencement list.
- The DIG starts the review. It does not replace any requirement and does not guarantee graduation: every requirement (including the Career Connected Experience for the Fall 2026 and Beyond pathway, and Honors requirements if you are in Honors) must still be met.
- Questions: your faculty advisor first, then ManningSchoolofBusiness@uml.edu. Exact deadlines each term are on the Academic Calendar: https://www.uml.edu/registrar/calendars/

Related links: Commencement "Who to Contact" https://www.uml.edu/commencement/contact.aspx ; commencement list https://www.uml.edu/resources/registrar/term_spring/graduation/grad_list.html ; Solution Center graduation page https://www.uml.edu/thesolutioncenter/academics/degree-program/graduate.aspx

**Agent notes (internal):**
Use whenever the student asks how/when to apply for graduation, mentions the DIG, asks about the commencement list, says it is their last or second-to-last semester, or the plan shows one semester left; also when the report shows 85+ earned credits and the student plans to finish next semester.
- Identify the student's final semester and say the DIG is submitted the semester before it, after registering for the final semester's courses. Never invent a deadline date; point to the Academic Calendar.
- Do not say the DIG guarantees graduation; say the college reviews and approves it.
- ENTR.1000 / ENTR.1500 / BUSI.1500 waivers are processed during the DIG review (see the Entrepreneurship intro course FAQ).
- Online and Professional Studies undergraduates use the GPS form (http://gps.uml.edu/policies/graduation.cfm). Master's and doctoral students are out of scope.
- When a plan fits in one more semester, add one line to the plan: "Submit your DIG this semester (one semester before your graduation term)."

### E.2 How many credits do I need to graduate?

**Answer:**
The university minimum for a bachelor's degree is 120 credits. Business students typically finish with about 120-122, depending on choices such as a 3-credit science course with the lab built in versus a 3-credit course plus a separate 1-credit lab. What matters most is that you meet every program requirement and reach at least 120 credits. If College Writing I is waived, you may need an extra elective to get to 120.

The "credits remaining" number on your advisement report can be misleading, especially for transfer students, so follow the requirement lists on the report instead.

**Agent notes (internal):**
120 is a floor: keep every required course even if the total goes over 120. The pathway notes in Part B list "Total Minimum Credits: 122" as the planning total for a standard pathway; do not present 122 as a university graduation rule.

### E.3 What GPA do I need to graduate?

**Answer:**
You need an overall GPA of at least 2.000 and a Manning (major courses) GPA of at least 2.200. Your advisement report opens the GPA sections when you are below either minimum, so check the report in SiS.

**Agent notes (internal):**
If the report does not show a GPA, say you cannot tell whether the student meets either minimum and suggest the advisor. Flag GPA below 2.0 / 2.2 per the main instructions.

### E.4 What GPA do I need to graduate with Academic Honors (Latin honors)?

**Answer:**
University Honors at graduation requires at least a 3.25 GPA for all courses completed at UMass Lowell and at least 60 credits earned at UMass Lowell. Up to 9 credits of departmental exam or S-graded courses can count toward those 60; Pass/Fail credits cannot. Transfer students also need at least a 3.0 at their previous institution and must meet the university residency requirement.

Distinction levels: Summa Cum Laude 3.85-4.00, Magna Cum Laude 3.500-3.849, Cum Laude 3.250-3.499.

**Agent notes (internal):**
This is different from the Honors College (Commonwealth Honors), which has its own 3.250 GPA and curriculum requirements.

### E.5 What is the most credits I can take in a semester?

**Answer:**
Pathways are built around about 15-16 credits a semester, and you can register for up to 20 credits without extra approval. To take more than 20, submit the online Undergraduate Academic Petition (Solution Center > Academic Forms: https://www.uml.edu/thesolutioncenter/Forms/Academic-Forms.aspx) and explain why you need the extra course(s). Approval depends on your past academic performance and exceptional circumstances, and requests are usually approved only up to 21 credits.

**Agent notes (internal):**
Never promise approval. When planning, stay within the 15-18 credit guidance in the main instructions.

### E.6 How many credits make me a full-time student?

**Answer:**
Undergraduates need at least 12 credits (usually four 3-credit courses) to be full-time, unless Disability Services has approved a reduced course load for you. International students may have limits on online and hybrid courses, so check with the International Students & Scholars Office (https://www.uml.edu/isso/contact/).

### E.7 Can I take a course Pass/No Credit?

**Answer:**
You can take up to four unrestricted elective courses on a Pass/No Credit basis. A pass-no credit course cannot count toward Core Curriculum (Breadth of Knowledge), major, minor, or collateral requirements. A P means C- or better and does not affect your GPA; an NC means the course was failed without affecting your cumulative average. You must request the grading option for each course by the last day to add/drop, and you cannot change it after that deadline.

### E.8 Can I delete a bad grade to improve my GPA?

**Answer:**
Students who entered UMass Lowell with fewer than 60 transfer credits can repeat or delete up to 15 credits of courses with grades of C- or lower. Students who entered with 60 or more transfer credits can repeat or delete up to 7 credits. To request a deletion, fill out the Course Deletion form (Solution Center > Academic Forms: https://www.uml.edu/thesolutioncenter/Forms/Academic-Forms.aspx).

### E.9 How do I drop a course?

**Answer:**
During the add/drop period you can drop a course yourself in SiS with no record on your transcript. After add/drop you may be able to withdraw until the last day to withdraw, which earns a grade of W. See the Withdrawal from Courses policy: https://www.uml.edu/catalog/undergraduate/policies/academic-policies/withdrawal-from-courses.aspx

**Agent notes (internal):**

## Concentrations, minors and advisors

### E.10 Can I have a dual (two) or triple (three) concentration?

**Answer:**
Dual: yes, as long as it fits within the degree requirements. You do not need two concentrations. Students complete 11 courses (33 credits) at the 3000/4000 level on top of the 5 core and senior core courses, plus 2 Manning or non-Manning electives. Most concentrations have 5 required courses (Accounting has 8 and Finance has 7), so two concentrations usually fit inside the 11 courses; for Accounting or Finance pairings you may use your free electives. SiS has a specific advisement report for every pairing of concentrations. Accounting + Finance together require 13 courses, because ACCT.3300 and ACCT.4010 count as approved Finance electives.

Triple: no. It would normally delay graduation and is not approved by the registrar. The Bachelor's-to-Master's (4+1) program is the alternative to explore.

**Agent notes (internal):**
For a pair other than Accounting + Finance, there is no combined overlap rule: check each concentration independently, schedule a shared course once, invent no overlap, and confirm the combined elective count with ManningSchoolofBusiness@uml.edu. Students have only one faculty advisor even with two concentrations.

### E.11 How do I change or add a concentration or a minor, and how can I see what it would change?

Update your concentration before your advising appointment; it typically takes one day to show on your advisement report in SiS. In SiS you can also run your own "What-If" report (select a new concentration, add or drop a second concentration, or add a minor) and save a PDF to compare. Video tutorial: https://www.uml.edu/thesolutioncenter/academics/degree-program/what-if.aspx

**Agent notes (internal):**

### E.12 Can I add a minor?

**Answer:**
Manning students can add a minor offered outside the business school, including interdisciplinary minors (list: https://www.uml.edu/Catalog/Undergraduate/Undergraduate-Minors.aspx). Contact the department program coordinator listed at the bottom of that minor's catalog page to add it. You must finish the minor on or before the date you complete your major: you cannot stay extra time to finish a minor, so plan the minor courses with that department early.

## Breadth of Knowledge and electives

### E.14 Can I take another course with the same prefix (for example a third POLI) for my Social Science, Science with Lab or Arts & Humanities requirement?

**Answer:**
No more than two Breadth of Knowledge electives can share a course prefix, and the limit is counted across your Arts & Humanities, Social Sciences and Science with Lab electives together, not within each category. Example: if you have two POLI courses (such as POLI.1010 and another POLI course), you cannot take a third POLI course for any of those requirements, so choose a different prefix (for Social Sciences, PSYC, SOCI or CRIM are other options). The same limit applies to HIST: no more than two HIST courses overall.

**Agent notes (internal):**
Procedure before suggesting or placing any AH, SS or SCL elective:
1. List the student's AH/SS/SCL elective courses (completed, in progress, and already planned) by prefix.
2. Count each prefix. A prefix at two is blocked for all three categories.
3. Recommend only courses from the Part A Section 6 lists with a different prefix, and tell the student plainly why the blocked prefix is not allowed.
4. Prior pathway: the required HIST elective counts toward the HIST limit.
5. If the report already shows more than two of a prefix, flag it and refer the student to their advisor.
Not stated in the knowledge base (do not guess; say so and refer to ManningSchoolofBusiness@uml.edu): whether required courses such as ENGL, MATH or ECON.2010 count toward the limit, and whether a science course's co-requisite lab counts as a second course with that prefix. When recommending a second science course, prefer a different prefix from the first.
The limit is stated for Breadth of Knowledge courses only; do not apply it to Manning or free electives.

## Entrepreneurship intro course, transfer credit and study abroad

### E.25 Do I need ENTR.1500, BUSI.1500 or ENTR.1000?

**Answer:**
ENTR.1500 replaced the old pair of ENTR.1000 and BUSI.1500 for students who joined the program in Spring 2021 or later. ENTR.1000 is no longer offered.

Joined Spring 2021 or later: if you entered with 30 or more credits, ENTR.1500 is waived during your DIG review (if that leaves you under 120 credits, you will need a free elective). If you entered with fewer than 30 credits, you need ENTR.1500; if you did not take it until junior or senior year, take a Manning 3-credit elective instead, preferably ENTR.3000 or another ENTR 3000/4000 course.

Entered Fall 2019 to Fall 2020 (BUSI.1500 era): 30 or more entering credits means BUSI.1500 is waived at your DIG review; fewer than 30 credits and not taken means take a Manning 3-credit elective, preferably ENTR.3000 or another ENTR 3000/4000 course.

Transfer students listed with ENTR.1000: since it is no longer offered, take ENTR.3000 or any ENTR 3000/4000 course for an elective, or take part in a DifferenceMaker competition to have ENTR.1000 waived. The waiver is applied during the DIG review or after the upper-level course grade posts, and is noted in your DIG confirmation letter.

In every case you still need at least 120 credits to graduate.

**Agent notes (internal):**
Combine with the main-instruction ENTR.1500 rule: if the student has 30+ credits earned or in progress before that semester and has not taken ENTR.1500, skip it and plan ENTR.3000. A waiver is requested via ManningSchoolofBusiness@uml.edu after the replacement course is complete.

**Answer:**

### E.31 What are Honors Ready sections, the E1 requirement and the difference between an honors thesis and project?

**Answer:**
Honors Ready: a regular section whose instructor prepares an honors component so honors and non-honors students share the class. Tell the instructor you want the honors component before the end of add/drop.

E1: one enrichment requirement per semester in the Honors College: attend an enriching event outside your major (or do service work) and submit a short response paper.

Thesis vs project: an Honors Thesis tests a hypothesis with new research and is written like an academic article (6 credits over two semesters). An Honors Project is everything else (6 credits over two semesters or 3 credits in one semester). Both need a final archival document. You can complete the project or thesis as a directed study with a Manning faculty member. Questions go to the Honors Office (honors@uml.edu).

### E.32 How does a Co-op work and how many credits does it earn?

**Answer:**
Apply the semester before you want to enroll in the Professional Development Seminar (PDS); you can apply as early as the second semester of your first year. Co-op cycles: January-June (6 months), July-December (6 months), and June-August (3-month summer). Start at https://www.uml.edu/student-services/career-services/cooperative-education/ and attend an information session.

Manning credit sequence (3-month or 6-month): BUSI.2100 Professional Development Seminar (1 credit, free elective), COOP 3CE (0 credits), then the co-op assessment course (BUSI.3100 or BUSI.4100 for a 3-month co-op, BUSI.3200 or BUSI.4200 for a 6-month co-op; the second co-op uses the 4000-level course) for 3 credits that count as a Manning 3000/4000 elective. Many students complete two co-ops. Plan the sequence with your advisor.

**Agent notes (internal):**
BUSI.3100 and BUSI.4100 are now 3 credits (the prerequisite table may still show 2 credits for BUSI.3100; if they conflict, tell the student to confirm credits in Class Search). Do not mention Part references.

### E.33 How does an internship for credit work?

**Answer:**
Juniors and seniors can take an internship for credit: a 4000-level, 3-credit course in the discipline (or the interdisciplinary BUSI.4890). Students generally need at least a 3.0 GPA. Get approval before you start: send the job description and supervisor contact to the instructor of the internship course (find the instructor in Class Search). The faculty member reviews the position (it should apply your discipline, include workplace mentoring, and usually involve about 10 or more hours a week), talks with your supervisor, and then gives you a permission number to register for the semester of the internship. Expect several updates during the semester and a final report. Internships are generally part-time, while a Co-op is a longer, structured experience across three semesters.

### E.45 I'm not sure where my next meal is coming from or I don't have stable housing.

**Answer:**
Please contact the Dean of Students' Office (2nd floor of University Crossing). For immediate food, the UMass Lowell Strive Pantry is in University Crossing, room 104, near the Solution Center; bring your student ID, and it is free (https://www.uml.edu/student-services/UML-Strive/Food-Pantry/default.aspx).

### E.46 I have a documented disability. Can I get accommodations?

**Answer:**
Register with Disability Services through the Accommodate platform (https://www.uml.edu/student-services/disability/students/how-to-register.aspx). Once registered, request your accommodation letters each semester (https://www.uml.edu/student-services/disability/students/semester-request.aspx), then tell each instructor as early as possible. Contact: disability@uml.edu or 978-934-4574.

**Agent notes (internal):**
If the student's report shows an overall GPA above 3.0 and open elective slots, mention that 4+1 courses may be possible and suggest speaking with the contacts above. Do not state the official eligibility GPA; send the student to the program page.
